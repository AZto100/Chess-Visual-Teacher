Stockfish's documented UCI_Elo starts at 1320 and is anchored to an engine rating list, not FIDE. Its built-in strength reduction deliberately chooses suboptimal moves. This board instead keeps that reduction off, so your Allow Blunder checkbox has sole control over intentional random move selection.

Same position, same best move? Often, yes. Fixed single-thread search is not shuffled automatically here. Allow Blunder adds variety by sampling the best four analysed moves, without changing the preset. With it off, the board always uses the engine's final best move; even equal-score ties are not deliberately randomised. Different human opening moves also lead to different games.

The hash/search cache is cleared before each search so changing from a stronger to a weaker preset does not reuse deeper analysis. The optional time cap may stop a search before its full budget is reached. The completed depth is shown below the hints. These limits apply in Practice too, so low-level hints are not full-strength analysis.

Allow Blunder and move probabilities

Unchecked: use Stockfish's final bestmove, with no intentional random choice. This is the best move found within the selected search budget, not a promise that it is objectively perfect. The weighting selector is disabled.

Checked (default): preserve the existing weighted draw among the four best candidates. Allowing a lower-ranked move does not force a blunder, and the top four may all be good moves.

Both choices retain your rank bonuses: 20%, 10%, 5%, 0%, then normalise the preliminary weights to total 100%. Percentages mean chance of selecting a move, not chance of winning.

Score-gap: using scores in pawn units from the moving side's viewpoint, calculate w = exp((score - bestScore) / 1.0). The preliminary weight is rankBonus + w / sum(w). This keeps the score order intact with positive, zero, or negative evaluations.

Your formula: use rankBonus + score / totalScore. When the signed total is zero or negative, use the sum of absolute scores as the denominator; if all scores are zero, start with equal shares. If any preliminary weight is negative, add the same amount to all so the smallest becomes 0.05, then normalise. That 5% floor is before normalising, not a minimum final chance.

Mate safeguard: take an available immediate checkmate; otherwise favour forced-mate lines over non-mating lines. Avoid an engine-reported forced loss when another candidate avoids it. Within an all-mate pool, use mate distance rather than pretending mate is a centipawn score. Fewer than four legal moves are handled automatically.

The computer waits for its search to end (depth, node budget or time limit) before choosing. A brief pause makes the reply visible. Changing mode, flipping, undoing or starting a new game cancels a pending choice.

All analysis runs in this browser. Keep vendor/chess.js and engine/stockfish-19-lite-single.js with its matching WASM file. No external network requests at runtime.
